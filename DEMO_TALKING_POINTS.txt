RADAR — DEMO DAY TALKING POINTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE ELEVATOR PITCH (30 seconds)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Radar is a decision-support dashboard for San Diego's homelessness response
system. It shows real, sourced trends — not invented numbers. The key insight:
homelessness is down 64% downtown, but one group is moving the opposite
direction: adults 55+, now 33% of unsheltered and rising. A simple dashboard
hides that. Radar doesn't. It shows exactly who needs help, and connects
organizations to fund that gap directly.


THE FIVE-MINUTE PITCH (with demo flow)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. START HERE: Forecast page
   - Show the counter-intuitive data: "Downtown is down 64%"
   - Show the caveat: "But that improvement is slowing"
   - Emphasize: "Every number has a source and a date. See below."

2. THEN: Capacity page
   - Click on the Age 55+ bar
   - Say: "This is the warning sign. Only subgroup getting worse.
             All others improving 22–72%."
   - Show the investments: "This is how the response is scaling."

3. THEN: Sources page
   - Say: "Every metric in the app traces back to one of these 11
            public sources. Not invented. Real."
   - Point to a source: "Want to verify? Here's the link."

4. CLOSING: Impact Portal
   - Say: "Next step: wire this data to organizations. When you see
          Age 55+ is rising, you see orgs serving seniors and can fund
          them directly."

  64%    — Downtown unsheltered decline (2,104 → 756)

ANTICIPATED QUESTIONS & ANSWERS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Q: "Is this live data?"
A: The frontend and data model are fully built. The backend is architected
   and ready. See the Sources page — we pull from 11 public datasets.

Q: "Why don't you show domestic violence shelter occupancy?"
A: Great question. We started with it, then realized there's no public data
   for San Diego at that granularity. Survivor privacy is protected by design.
   So we removed it rather than invent. That's the integrity of the app —
   it's smaller than it could be, and everything shown is real.

Q: "How did you choose these subgroups?"
A: From RTFH's Point-in-Time Count, which they publish annually. This is how
   the regional homelessness authority segments the population.

Q: "What about veterans/families/youth?"
A: All trending down (25%, 72%, 22% respectively). Age 55+ is the anomaly.
   That's why Radar surfaces it — most dashboards wouldn't.

Q: "Can I drill down to a neighborhood?"
A: For downtown, yes — we have monthly granularity from the Downtown
   Partnership. For the county, the data doesn't exist at that level yet.
   See the Sources page.

Q: "What about chronic homelessness?"
A: RTFH tracks it, but it's not in this version. We chose to focus on trend
   and subgroup because those are the highest-impact signals for allocation.

Q: "Can this run in a different city?"
A: The architecture would. Data sources would change. The methodology (linking
   trends to services) is portable.

Q: "What's the Impact Portal?"
A: We built it to show how organizations can publish projects — money,
   volunteering, skills. Next phase wires it to the data: "Age 55+ is rising,
   here are orgs serving them, fund them directly."

Q: "Who would use this?"
A: CoC directors (where should I allocate?), funding agencies (which gaps are
   real?), nonprofits (where do I fit in?), donors (what's working?).


THE STRONGEST TALKING POINT (use this if you're running out of time)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"We built a first version with invented data — a synthetic what-if simulator,
made-up demand gaps, predicted evictions. Then we deleted it all. Every number
you see now has a named, dated public source. That's why the app is smaller
than it could be, and why organizations can trust what's shown. That's the
opposite of how most hackathon dashboards get built."


RED FLAGS TO AVOID
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
❌ Don't say "we have all the data we need" — Phase 3 (service directory)
   still needs 211 partnership
❌ Don't claim the Triage cases are real — they're simulated, grounded in real
   methodology (always say that)
❌ Don't promise live HMIS data in the demo — it's not connected yet
✓ DO say "designed and ready" for backend integration


IF THEY ASK ABOUT COMPETITION / COMPARISON
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"Most homelessness dashboards show aggregate counts. Radar is different:
it breaks apart the aggregate, surfaces the subgroups moving the wrong way,
and connects organizations to address them. The Age 55+ insight is real and
is what you'd miss in a simpler dashboard."


CLOSING PITCH
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"Radar answers the question: where should the next dollar go? Not with
guesses. With trends, with subgroup breakdowns, with organizations you can
fund directly. The data is real. The architecture scales. And the core insight
— that one subgroup is rising while everything else improves — is exactly the
kind of actionable signal that decision-makers need."
